# Lead temperature: cold, warm, hot, customer

A lead's temperature says how close they are to buying, judged only from what
they did, never from a hunch. Gather the evidence first, then apply these rules.

| Temperature | The evidence |
|---|---|
| **Cold** | Found by a search, or contacted with no reply. Fit and a trigger make a cold lead worth contacting; they don't make it warm. |
| **Warm** | They engaged: replied with interest, a question, an objection or a referral; or engaged with the founder's posts or messages; or the founder has already been in touch with them before. |
| **Hot** | They want to talk: asked for a meeting, agreed a time, or have a meeting on the founder's calendar. |
| **Customer** | They have bought: a won deal in the founder's CRM, or the founder says so. |

## How it moves
- **Up** only on new evidence, and always with the reason and date ("Replied
  asking about pricing, Nov 8").
- **Not now** keeps a lead warm but quiet until the date they gave.
- **Unsubscribe** or "not interested" means do-not-contact, whatever the
  temperature was.
- **Cooling:** warm with no activity for about 30 days drifts back to cold. Hot
  with no meeting for about 30 days drifts back to warm.
- **Out of office** changes nothing.

## Focus
When the founder asks who to work on: hot first, then warm with a fresh signal,
then follow-ups that are due, then new cold leads with a strong fit and a
recent trigger. Always say why each one is on the list.

## Never
- Call a lead warm because they fit well or recently raised money: that is a
  good cold lead.
- Contact someone already a customer or in an open deal as if they were cold.
