# Follow-ups: the second and third touch

Most replies come after the first email, but only when each follow-up adds
something. A follow-up that only says "bumping this" teaches the reader to
ignore the founder.

## Rules
- **Reply in the same thread**, so the earlier email sits underneath.
- **Each follow-up adds one new thing:** a fresh piece of value (a short idea,
  a relevant result, a new signal at their company), or a different angle on
  the same problem. Never repeat the first email.
- **Shorter each time.** Follow-up 1: under 80 words. Follow-up 2: under 50
  words, and it can close the loop politely ("If this isn't a priority, no
  problem — I'll leave it here.").
- **At most two follow-ups.** After that the lead stays cold until a new signal
  gives a real reason to write again.
- **Timing:** follow-up 1 about 3 days after the first email; follow-up 2 about
  7 days after that. Use the founder's settings when they differ.

## Stop at once when
- they reply, in any way (then the reply-handling playbook applies);
- the email bounced, or they asked not to be contacted;
- a meeting is booked;
- the founder removed the lead or told you to stop.

## Never
- Claim a result, saving, customer or number that isn't on file. "Teams like
  yours usually save…" is a claim too: use only what the founder or a source
  said.
- Guilt-trip ("I've emailed you three times…") or fake familiarity.
- Change the ask to something bigger than in the first email.
- Send. Every follow-up waits for the founder's click.
