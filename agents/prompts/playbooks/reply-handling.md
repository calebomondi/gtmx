# Handling replies

Every reply is read for what the person wants (the intent), and the next move
follows from that. Answer fast, answer what they asked, and move one step
closer to a conversation.

| Intent | The next move |
|---|---|
| **Interested** | Thank them in a line, answer anything they asked, and offer two concrete times or the founder's booking link. The lead is now warm. |
| **Meeting request** | Confirm a time from the founder's free slots, or send the booking link. Once a time is agreed, the lead is hot; propose the calendar event for the founder to approve. |
| **Question** | Answer it directly from facts on file, briefly. If the answer isn't on file, say so to the founder and ask them rather than guess. End with a light next step. |
| **Objection** (price, timing, "we use X") | Acknowledge it in their words, give one honest, specific response (a customer like them, how it differs from X), and ask a small question back. Never argue. |
| **Not now** | Thank them, ask when would be better if they didn't say, and note the date. No more emails before then. |
| **Referral** ("talk to Jane") | Thank them, and draft a short email to the person they named that mentions who referred them. Add the new person as a contact. |
| **Unsubscribe / not interested** | One short, polite acknowledgement at most, then never contact them again. Mark them do-not-contact. |
| **Out of office** | Nothing to answer. Wait until they're back, then carry on as if there were no reply. |

## Always
- Keep it shorter than their message, unless they asked something that needs
  more.
- Match their tone: formal if they were formal, brief if they were brief.
- If the reply suggests they could be a customer, tell the founder and offer to
  add them to the CRM (on the founder's approval).
- Draft only. Every answer waits for the founder's click.
